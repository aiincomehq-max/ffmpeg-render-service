# 50 hooks they use on you

**What this is.** A field guide to how "AI made me money" posts are built, written by someone who built the thing they describe: a self-hosted automation server, 600+ workflows, a couple of hundred by hand, thirty products in 140 days, and £0. Every pattern here has been seen in the wild. None of it is about any one creator. All of it is about the shape of the post. The list grows by one or two with every Receipts vs Claims episode.

Use it as a checklist. If a post hits five or more of these, you are looking at a funnel, and the number in it is doing a job.

## The number

1. **The impossible number.** A result so large it stops the scroll, stated once, with nothing that lets you check it.
2. **The precise number.** $7,526, not $7,500. Precision reads as honesty; it is free to type.
3. **The blank column.** Views and revenue shown; hours, ad spend, refunds and audience size missing. You fill the blank with zero.
4. **The chosen rectangle.** A dashboard screenshot cropped to remove the date, the period and the account name.
5. **The single-post halo.** One outlier's views presented as the output of the system.
6. **Views as income.** A view count where a revenue figure would be, and your brain does the conversion.
7. **The unstated denominator.** "3 million views" with no count of channels, posts or months.
8. **The short window.** "In one week." Short windows make results sound repeatable when they are not.
9. **The round-trip claim.** "Made back the cost of the course in a day." The course is the product; the claim sells it.
10. **Borrowed scale.** "My client did $50k." Not their result; not checkable; inherits the size anyway.

## The frame

11. **Borrowed authority.** "I gave my page to Claude." The brand does the work, so the claim inherits the brand's credibility.
12. **The effortless frame.** "I just asked it to…" The labour is deleted from the sentence so you picture none.
13. **The five prompts.** A complex system reduced to a short list, so the barrier appears to be knowledge, which they sell.
14. **The stack as product.** The tool list is the deliverable; the links behind it are affiliate links.
15. **The before-me.** "Six months ago I was broke." A low starting point makes any result look like a system.
16. **The confession.** "I was doing it wrong for a year." Vulnerability lowers your guard; the fix is the product.
17. **Green-tick proof.** A workflow canvas full of successful runs, shown as if execution were outcome.
18. **The montage.** Fast cuts of AI output implying volume; volume is not reach.
19. **The "fully automated" promise.** Never true at the edges; the edges are where the hours go.
20. **The "no face, no voice" promise.** Removes the thing you are afraid of, so you stop asking what replaces it.

## The visual

21. **Handwritten paper.** Reads as a note to a friend; costs nothing; hides the polish.
22. **The phone-filmed screen.** Pointing a phone at a laptop reads as candid and unedited.
23. **The dashboard as proof.** A UI element borrowed for credibility; a UI can display any number.
24. **The notification stack.** Screenshots of payment notifications, stacked; dates and names cropped.
25. **Face to camera in a car.** Parasocial trust plus the implication of a life with no office.
26. **The whiteboard.** Teacher framing; you are the student; the lesson is paid.
27. **The "I'm not selling anything" card.** Said in the video, contradicted by the bio link.
28. **Text on a plain background.** Speed and volume signal; hundreds a day; the numbers are unverified by design.
29. **The screen-pan.** A quick pan over a system, too fast to read, slow enough to impress.
30. **The two-column comparison.** "Old way / AI way" with the old way made deliberately painful.

## The funnel

31. **The keyword gate.** "Comment X." Turns engagement into lead capture and reach at the same time.
32. **The exact steps.** Specificity promised, delivered as a video that sells the specifics.
33. **The free training.** A webinar whose last twenty minutes are the pitch.
34. **The follow gate.** "Follow and I'll send it," which the bot enforces.
35. **The urgency line.** "Only sending this for 24 hours." The bot does not have a clock.
36. **The DM opener.** "Hey, are you serious about this?" A qualification question that starts a sales script.
37. **The community.** A paid group whose value is other people who also paid.
38. **The template drop.** A free workflow file that requires the paid one to run.
39. **The audit call.** A "free strategy call" that is a sales call with a diagnosis attached.
40. **The scarcity of spots.** "Five spots left" on a digital product.

## The you

41. **The mirror.** "If you're tired of…" describes you exactly; it describes everyone.
42. **The enemy.** "Your boss / the 9 to 5 / the gurus." An outside villain to move you closer to the poster.
43. **The permission.** "You're allowed to want this." Emotional framing before a financial ask.
44. **The countdown to regret.** "In a year you'll wish you started today."
45. **The identity flip.** "Builders vs consumers." You buy to become the former.
46. **The proof by comments.** "Look how many people are asking." The comments were manufactured by the keyword gate.
47. **The reluctant reveal.** "I wasn't going to share this." Withheld value reads as valuable.
48. **The insider frame.** "What they don't want you to know." No one is hiding it; it is on every feed.
49. **The success stack.** Three testimonials, first names only, results unverifiable.
50. **The final line.** "Comment the word." You are the engine; your comment is the reach.

## What to do with it

Count the hits. Then look for the four things every honest claim carries and these posts leave out: the hours, the cost, the time frame and the denominator. If all four are missing, the number is doing a job. Mine are in the Ledger, and the Ledger is free.
