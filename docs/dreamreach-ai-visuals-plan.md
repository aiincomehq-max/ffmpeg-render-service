### The formula, from watching his top two reels scene by scene

**TRAPPY (22.7K) — Trapstar brand film, 24s**

- **Structure:** 4s of dialogue and story, then the beat drops into fast detail cuts, then a line of dialogue to close and the credit.

### His 7 rules (copy these)
1. **Make it for someone with an audience**: a rapper, streetwear, jewellery, or car brand. Tag them in the caption *and* on the video.
2. **The first 2 seconds are dialogue or a striking object**, never a logo or title card.
3. **A nostalgic, cinematic world** (UK 90s/Y2K, 90s NYC). It reads as "expensive", not "AI".
4. **Hide the brand inside the world** rather than slapping it on. That gives people a reason to rewatch.
5. **20–45s length, a cut every 1.5–3s, synced to the beat.**
6. **The same sign-off every time** ("BY BDOT."). It builds a recognisable maker's mark.
7. **Keep the caption short:** title, credit, the tool tag, 1–3 hashtags. Tagging the tool (@higgsfield.ai) is also what got him the partnership.

### What not to copy
- His posting rate (about one a day) is affordable because his credits are covered. You need fewer, better-aimed videos.
- His untagged original concepts underperform. **Every DreamReach video should have a target account.**

### Production pipeline (every shot animated)
1. **Stills first:** generate the 12 keyframe images below. Images are the cheap step, so re-roll each one until it's right **before** animating. For the logo shots, give the image model the **GRM Daily logo as a reference image** so the shape comes out correctly.
2. **Animate every shot:** run image-to-video on all 12 stills with the motion prompts below. Generate **5s clips** and trim each to its 1–3s slot in the edit, using the best 2s of motion.
3. **Credit maths:** 12 clips, plus about 4 re-rolls for the shots that come out wrong, is **about 16 video generations per video**. Check the per-clip cost on whichever Higgsfield plan you pick and make sure one month covers about 3 videos (about 50 generations).
4. **Voice:** generate 2 dialogue lines in ElevenLabs (a London male voice).
5. **Assemble in `ffmpeg-render-service`** with a beat-synced cut, grain overlay, end card and audio mix (needs the upgrades in section 6).
6. **Run a virality check** before posting (Higgsfield `virality_predictor` or vidIQ) to compare hook options.

## 6. Render service: DONE (upgraded and tested)
`app.py` now supports:
- **Per-clip length:** `duration_s` and `trim_start_s`, or `bpm` + `beats_per_shot` to cut on the beat. Images are accepted as well as video.
- **Audio:** a `music_url` with `music_start_s`, several `voice_lines` at set times, and automatic **ducking** of the music under the voice. The video is never cut short by a short audio file.
- **Looks:** `grain`, `vignette`, and per-clip `look`: `"cctv"` (fisheye, timestamp, noise) or `"camcorder"` (4:3 inside 9:16, a blinking REC dot, date).
- **Text:** overlays and the **"DREAMREACH." end card** (`end_card`). Quotes and colons in text are safe.
- **Download:** `GET /render/{job_id}` returns the finished MP4.
- **Old payloads** still work exactly as before.
- **Local files** are only read when `ALLOW_LOCAL_FILES=1`, so a public deployment can't be made to read the server's disk.

**Render on the Mac without running the server:**
```
python render_job.py jobs/bemz-dnd.json     # → out/bemz-dnd.mp4
```
It lists any shot files still missing before it renders.

**Files:**
- `jobs/*.json` contains the cut lists for all three videos.
- `prompts/*.md` has the copy-paste image and motion prompts for every shot, plus the file name to save each clip as.
- `assets/<video>/` is where the clips go (named `01.mp4`, `02.mp4`, …).

**On the Mac, once:** `pip install -r requirements.txt` (and `brew install ffmpeg` if you don't have it).
