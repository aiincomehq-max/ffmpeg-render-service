### The formula, from watching his top two reels scene by scene

**TRAP SCHOLAR (39.8K) — rapper visual, 43s**
- **Hook (0–2s):** a crew staring down the lens, with spoken dialogue: *"What you looking at, blood? This is how we do it round here."* Spoken words come before any music.
- **World:** 90s/Y2K UK nostalgia. Barbershop, car wash, petrol station at night, dominoes in a living room, estate courtyard.
- **Flex contrast:** an ordinary UK setting next to a Ferrari F40, a Rolls-Royce drift, a Rolex on a velvet tray.
- **Grade:** film-stock look. Grain, halation, crushed blacks, warm skin tones.
- **Cut rate:** a new shot every 1.5–3s, cut to the beat.
- **Credit:** a small yellow "BY BDOT" card on the last 2s only. No other text.

**TRAPPY (22.7K) — Trapstar brand film, 24s**
- **Hook:** a gold "T" chain dangling out of a vintage Cadillac window in snowy 90s New York.
- **Mechanic:** the brand's logo is hidden in every shot: snow drift tracks, a window shadow, a cane handle, stitched seats, a hood ornament, a barber pole, road markings on Brooklyn Bridge. **People rewatch to spot them**, and rewatches are what the algorithm rewards most.
- **Structure:** 4s of dialogue and story, then the beat drops into fast detail cuts, then a line of dialogue to close and the credit.

### His 7 rules (copy these)
1. **Make it for someone with an audience**: a rapper, streetwear, jewellery, or car brand. Tag them in the caption *and* on the video.
2. **The first 2 seconds are dialogue or a striking object**, never a logo or title card.
3. **A nostalgic, cinematic world** (UK 90s/Y2K, 90s NYC). It reads as "expensive", not "AI".
4. **Hide the brand inside the world** rather than slapping it on. That gives people a reason to rewatch.
5. **20–45s length, a cut every 1.5–3s, synced to the beat.**
6. **The same sign-off every time** ("BY BDOT."). It builds a recognisable maker's mark.
7. **Keep the caption short:** title, credit, the tool tag, 1–3 hashtags. Tagging the tool (@higgsfield.ai) is also what got him the partnership.

### What not to copy
- His posting rate (about one a day) is affordable because his credits are covered. You need fewer, better-aimed videos.
- His untagged original concepts underperform. **Every DreamReach video should have a target account.**

## 4. VIDEO #1 PLAN — "GRM DAILY: BLOCK LORE" (hidden-logo brand film)

**Why this one first:** it combines his two winning formats: a music brand plus hidden logos. GRM Daily's audience is
UK rap fans *and* rappers, so if it gets a repost, rappers see DreamReach. You need no artist's song, likeness or permission to
make a spec piece. *(Swap the brand if you prefer. The same template works for Link Up TV or Trapstar-type labels.)*

- **Length:** 22–26s · **Format:** 9:16, 1080×1920 · **Look:** 2000s London, sodium streetlights at night, film grain, a teal and orange grade
- **Audio:** a royalty-free UK drill/grime instrumental (a 140 BPM drill beat from YouTube Audio Library or Pixabay) plus 2 lines of AI voice dialogue (ElevenLabs, which you already have connected)
- **Hidden logo:** the **GRM "G"/wordmark shape** worked into objects in each shot

### Low-cost production pipeline
1. **Stills first (cheap):** generate 12 keyframe images, one per shot, in an image model. That's the cheapest step, so re-roll stills until each one is right.
2. **Animate only the moving shots:** shots **1, 3, 6 and 11** as 5s image-to-video clips. Shots 2, 4, 5, 7, 8, 9 and 10 can be stills with a slow push-in/zoom done in ffmpeg (free).
   → about **4 video generations instead of 12**, roughly a third of the cost of doing them all as video.
3. **Voice:** generate 2 dialogue lines in ElevenLabs (a London male voice).
4. **Assemble in `ffmpeg-render-service`** with a beat-synced cut, grain overlay, end card and audio mix (needs the upgrades in section 6).
5. **Run a virality check** before posting (Higgsfield `virality_predictor` or vidIQ) to compare hook options.

**Higgsfield, if you do subscribe:** take the cheapest monthly plan for one month only, use it just for the 4 animated shots, and cancel if Video #1–#3 don't get a repost.

## 5. 30-day cadence (after Video #1)
- **3 videos a week**, rotating: 1 brand film (Tier A), 1 rapper visual (Tier B), 1 jewellery/car/streetwear brand film.
- Every video has a tag, the "DREAMREACH." sign-off, and a DM.

- **Once a reel passes 10K**, pin it and start pitching paid work: *"£150–£400 for a 20s AI visual for your next single"*. That's how BDOT got to the Shenseea job.

### Rights and safety (keep the accounts alive)
- Always label it **"unofficial / spec"**. Never claim to be hired by them.
- **No AI copies of a real rapper's face** without their OK. Use a backs-to-camera, masked or stand-in crew and let the *song* carry the artist. If they repost or reply, offer a version with their likeness.
- **Their song as audio:** use IG/TikTok's in-app library version of the track (it's licensed there). Don't upload the MP3 with the video file. YouTube will claim it, so use the instrumental or royalty-free beat on YT.

## 6. Terminal tasks (for the Mac session)
The repo's `app.py` render service needs these changes before it can assemble Video #1:

1. **Clip duration is hard-coded to 1.5s.** Add a `duration_s` per clip (accept still images too) plus an optional Ken Burns push-in for stills.
2. **No music track.** Add `music_url` and mix it with `voiceover_url` (ducking the music under the dialogue).
3. **The finished video can't be retrieved.** `/render` returns a `job_id`, but nothing serves `final.mp4`. Add a `GET /render/{job_id}` download endpoint, or upload the file and return a URL.
4. **Look:** add an optional film-grain + vignette filter, plus an end card (text, font and colour) for the "DREAMREACH." sign-off.
5. **Beat sync:** optionally take a BPM and snap cut points to the beat (140 BPM ≈ a cut every 1.71s, 2 beats per shot).
6. Then write a `jobs/block-lore.json` with the 12-shot list above and test a render.
